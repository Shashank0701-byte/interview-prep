LEADERSHIP AND MANAGEMENT INTERVIEW PREPARATION
===============================================

PRINCIPLES OF ENGINEERING LEADERSHIP
--------------------------------------

1. LEADERSHIP VS MANAGEMENT
- Leadership: setting vision, inspiring teams, creating culture, driving change
- Management: planning, organizing, staffing, controlling, budgeting
- Engineering managers manage execution; tech leads manage technical direction
- Leadership is a behavior, not a title - anyone can lead from any position
- Situational leadership: adapting style based on team maturity and context

2. BUILDING HIGH-PERFORMANCE TEAMS
- Psychological safety: creating environment where people take risks without fear
- Team formation (Tuckman model): Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing
- Cognitive diversity: different thinking styles and perspectives on teams
- Inclusion and belonging: ensuring all team members feel valued and heard
- Remote/hybrid team dynamics: async communication, written culture, trust building
- Team health metrics: deployment frequency, lead time, MTTR, change failure rate

3. TECHNICAL STRATEGY AND VISION
- Technology radar: identifying emerging technologies and their potential impact
- Build vs buy decisions: evaluating build costs, maintenance burden, vendor lock-in
- Technical debt management: categorizing, quantifying, prioritizing repayment
- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs): documenting context and rationale
- Long-term vs short-term trade-offs: speed vs quality, innovation vs stability
- Migration strategies: strangler fig pattern, parallel run, big bang

PEOPLE MANAGEMENT AND DEVELOPMENT
----------------------------------

1. MANAGING INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTORS
- 1-on-1 meetings: cadence, structure, feedback loops, career conversations
- Goal setting: OKRs (Objectives and Key Results), SMART goals, personal development plans
- Performance reviews: continuous feedback vs annual reviews, 360-degree feedback
- Growth paths: IC track (Senior, Staff, Principal, Fellow) vs Management track
- Skill development: training budgets, conference attendance, internal mobility
- Motivation: intrinsic vs extrinsic, autonomy-mastery-purpose framework

2. COACHING AND MENTORSHIP
- Coaching vs mentoring vs counseling: knowing the difference and when to use each
- Socratic questioning: asking questions that lead to insights rather than giving answers
- Situational coaching: Directive (tell), Supportive (ask), Delegative (let explore)
- Growth mindset: praising effort and strategy, not intelligence or talent
- Feedback models: SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact), COIN (Context-Observation-Impact-Next)
- Performance improvement plans (PIPs): when and how to use them effectively

3. DEALING WITH DIFFICULT SITUATIONS
- Conflict resolution: addressing tension directly, mediation techniques
- Low performance: diagnosing root cause (skill, will, or circumstance)
- Misalignment with company values: identifying and addressing gently but firmly
- Team restructuring: communicating changes, managing transition, handling resistance
- Layoffs and terminations: legal considerations, communication dignity, support programs
- Burnout prevention: workload management, well-being initiatives, boundary setting

ORGANIZATIONAL DESIGN AND CHANGE
---------------------------------

1. TEAM STRUCTURES
- Conway's Law: communication structures mirror system architecture
- Spotify model: squads, tribes, chapters, guilds; adaptability to different orgs
- Functional vs product vs matrix structures: pros and cons of each
- Cross-functional teams: reducing dependencies, increasing velocity
- Center of Excellence (CoE): shared platform teams, infrastructure teams, SRE teams
- Scaling teams: Dunbar's number, two-pizza teams, reverse Conway maneuver

2. AGILE METHODOLOGIES AT SCALE
- SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework): SAFe, LeSS, Nexus comparisons
- Scrum at scale: Scrum of Scrums, MetaScrum, Scrum@Scale
- Kanban: flow metrics (WIP, cycle time, throughput), service classes
- Agile metrics: velocity trends, cumulative flow diagram, control chart
- Retrospectives: start-stop-continue, 4Ls (Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for)
- Sprint planning vs continuous planning: trade-offs for different team types

3. CHANGE MANAGEMENT
- Kotter's 8-step change model: urgency, coalition, vision, communicate, empower, quick wins, consolidate, institutionalize
- ADKAR model: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement
- Resistance management: understanding sources of resistance, addressing concerns
- Incremental vs transformational change: when to use each approach
- Communication: frequency, channels, transparency, two-way feedback loops
- Metrics: adoption rate, satisfaction surveys, productivity changes

PRODUCT SENSE AND STRATEGY
---------------------------

1. PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT LIFECYCLE
- Discovery phase: user research, problem validation, market analysis, competitive landscape
- Definition phase: PRDs, user stories, acceptance criteria, success metrics
- Design phase: wireframes, prototypes, design reviews, usability testing
- Development phase: sprint planning, technical design, code reviews, testing
- Launch phase: rollout strategy, feature flags, monitoring, gradual deployment
- Iteration phase: A/B testing, metrics analysis, continuous improvement

2. DATA-DRIVEN DECISION MAKING
- North Star metric: single metric capturing customer value (e.g., MAUs, retention, NPS)
- Leading vs lagging indicators: predictive metrics vs outcome metrics
- Experimentation: A/B testing, multivariate testing, bandit algorithms
- Cohort analysis: retention curves, engagement patterns, user segmentation
- Funnel analysis: conversion rates, drop-off points, optimization opportunities
- Statistical significance: sample size calculation, confidence intervals, practical significance

3. STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT
- Identifying stakeholders: power-interest grid, stakeholder mapping
- Communication plans: who needs to know what, when, and how often
- Managing expectations: under-promise and over-deliver, transparent communication
- Executive summaries: pyramid principle, Minto method, top-line messaging
- Cross-team dependencies: dependency tracking, coordination meetings
- Dealing with difficult stakeholders: finding common ground, building trust

ENGINEERING CULTURE AND PRACTICES
----------------------------------

1. CODE REVIEW CULTURE
- Code review checklist: correctness, readability, performance, security, testing
- Review velocity: SLAs for review turnaround time, batch vs incremental reviews
- Giving effective feedback: specific, actionable, kind, timely
- Receiving feedback: growth mindset, separating ego from code
- Automated code review: static analysis, linters, formatters, CI checks
- Pair programming vs mob programming: when to use each technique

2. QUALITY AND TESTING
- Testing pyramid: unit tests, integration tests, contract tests, E2E tests, manual QA
- Test-driven development (TDD): red-green-refactor cycle, benefits and challenges
- CI/CD pipeline design: stages, gates, deployment strategies, rollback mechanisms
- Chaos engineering: principles, exercises, blameless postmortems
- Production testing: feature flags, canary releases, A/B testing in production
- Test flakiness: detection, quarantining, root cause analysis, fixing

3. ON-CALL AND INCIDENT MANAGEMENT
- Incident severity levels: SEV1 (critical), SEV2 (major), SEV3 (minor)
- Incident response: detection, triage, mitigation, resolution, follow-up
- Blameless postmortems: focusing on systemic issues, not individual mistakes
- SLO error budgets: using budget consumption to balance reliability and velocity
- On-call practices: rotation schedules, escalation policies, follow-the-sun
- Runbooks: documenting common incidents, troubleshooting steps, resolution procedures

INTERVIEW TECHNIQUES FOR LEADERSHIP ROLES
------------------------------------------

1. BEHAVIORAL FRAMEWORKS
- STAR-L: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Learning
- PARADE: Problem, Anticipated impact, Required actions, Actions taken, Development needed, Evaluate/measure
- XYZ: Accomplished X by doing Y resulting in Z (quantified format)
- CAR: Challenge, Action, Result (focused on personal contribution)
- SOARA: Situation, Obstacle, Action, Result, Assessment (for complex situations)

2. COMMON LEADERSHIP INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
"Tell me about a time you led a team through a difficult project."
Framework: STAR-L
Key areas: vision setting, stakeholder alignment, team motivation, overcoming obstacles

"Describe a situation where you had to deal with a low-performing team member."
Framework: PARADE
Key areas: diagnosis, action plan, feedback delivery, outcome (including PIP if used)

"Tell me about a time you influenced without authority."
Framework: XYZ
Key areas: relationship building, data presentation, coalition building, negotiated outcome

"Describe a significant technical decision you made and how you communicated it."
Framework: CAR
Key areas: ADR documentation, trade-off analysis, stakeholder buy-in, implementation

"How do you stay technically current while managing?"
Framework: STAR-L
Key areas: learning practices, time management, delegation, mentorship

3. ENGINEERING MANAGER CASE STUDIES
Case: "Your team is underperforming on deliverables. What do you do?"
Analysis: Diagnose root cause (skills, motivation, process, scope, dependencies)
Approach: Data collection (velocity metrics, 1-on-1s, stakeholder feedback)
Action plan: Specific interventions based on diagnosis
Success metrics: Improved delivery predictably, team satisfaction

Case: "You're asked to reduce team headcount by 20%. How do you approach it?"
Analysis: Business rationale, team structure, critical skills, legal considerations
Approach: Communication strategy, individual support, transition plans
Action plan: RIF/RR decisions, knowledge retention, team morale

Case: "Two senior engineers have a fundamental technical disagreement. How do you resolve it?"
Analysis: Technical merits of each position, team impact, business implications
Approach: Structured debate, data-driven evaluation, decision framework
Resolution: Document decision, communicate rationale, monitor outcomes

4. STAFF+ ENGINEER BEHAVIORAL QUESTIONS
"Describe a project you initiated that had org-wide impact."
Framework: STAR-L with emphasis on leadership (not just execution)
Focus: Vision, influencing, cross-team collaboration, measurable outcomes

"Tell me about a time you mentored another engineer through a difficult growth phase."
Framework: PARADE
Focus: Coaching approach, specific techniques used, observable growth

"How have you contributed to engineering culture at your organization?"
Framework: CAR
Focus: Concrete initiatives (onboarding docs, internal tools, guilds, standards)

"Describe a time you had to say no to a business request."
Framework: XYZ
Focus: Prioritization framework, data presentation, alternative solutions

LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES FROM TOP TECH COMPANIES
----------------------------------------------

1. AMAZON LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES
- Customer Obsession: Start with the customer and work backwards
- Ownership: Think long-term, never say "that's not my job"
- Invent and Simplify: Expect and require innovation from everywhere
- Learn and Be Curious: Never stop learning, explore new possibilities
- Hire and Develop the Best: Raise the performance bar with every hire
- Insist on Highest Standards: Continually raise the bar for quality
- Think Big: A small mindset is a self-fulfilling prophecy
- Bias for Action: Speed matters in business, calculated risk taking
- Frugality: Accomplish more with less, waste is a sin
- Dive Deep: Stay connected to details, audit frequently
- Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit: Disagree with data, commit once decided
- Deliver Results: Focus on key inputs, deliver with quality and timeliness

2. GOOGLE LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES
- Focus on the User: Understand user needs, make their lives better
- Think 10x: Aim for 10x improvement, not 10% improvement
- Technical Excellence: Hire the best, maintain high engineering standards
- Data Driven: Make decisions based on data, not opinion
- Cross-Functional Collaboration: Work across team boundaries effectively
- Psychological Safety: Create environment where people feel safe to take risks
- Fail Fast: Experiment quickly, learn from failures, iterate

3. NETFLIX CULTURE PRINCIPLES
- Freedom and Responsibility: Give employees freedom, expect high responsibility
- Context, not Control: Provide context, then trust people to make decisions
- Highly Aligned, Loosely Coupled: Align on goals, minimize dependencies
- Pay Top of Market: Retain the best talent with competitive compensation
- Promote Talent Density: Only keep highly effective contributors
- Radical Candor: Care personally while challenging directly
- Sunshining: Transparency and open communication across the organization
- Seek Feedback: Regularly ask for and act on feedback
- Lead with Context: Explain the "why" behind decisions
- Disagree Transparently: Different opinions are healthy, resolve them openly

COMMON LEADERSHIP MISTAKES TO AVOID
-------------------------------------
1. Micromanaging: delegating tasks but not trust
2. Not delegating enough: trying to do everything yourself
3. Avoiding difficult conversations: letting problems fester
4. Inconsistent feedback: only giving feedback during performance reviews
5. Not investing in team growth: focusing only on delivery
6. Making decisions without input: ignoring team expertise
7. Changing direction too frequently: creating churn and uncertainty
8. Not celebrating wins: missing opportunities for team motivation
9. Taking credit for team work: eroding trust and motivation
10. Not advocating for the team: failing to communicate team needs upward
